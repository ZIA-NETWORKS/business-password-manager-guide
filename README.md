# business-password-manager-guide
A plain-English guide to replacing sticky notes and password spreadsheets with a business password manager. Checklists and rollout tips from Zia Networks.
Why This Guide Exists

Walk through almost any small or mid-sized office, and you’ll spot it: a sticky note on a monitor with the Wi-Fi password, or a notebook labelled “admin logins” by the keyboard. Somewhere on the shared drive, there’s a file called Passwords_FINAL_v2.xlsx.

At Zia Networks, we see this in businesses of every size. It’s almost never because people are careless. They’re busy, they juggle dozens of accounts, and nobody gave them a better system. This guide explains why that has to change and how to do it without drama.

The Sticky Note Problem
Anyone can read them. Visitors, cleaners, delivery staff, even someone glancing at your background on a video call.
They never expire. The password stays put long after the employee has left.
They get lost. One office tidy-up and nobody can log into the accounting software.

A sticky note has no access control and no history. You’ll never know who saw it or when.

The Spreadsheet Problem

A password spreadsheet is just a sticky note with extra steps.

It gets shared. One forwarded email and your credential list lives in someone’s inbox forever.
It isn’t properly encrypted. Copy it to a personal laptop or USB stick and anyone can open it.
There’s no audit trail. You can’t tell who viewed, copied, or changed a login.
Offboarding is painful. You have to guess which passwords a leaver knew and change them all by hand.
Passwords get reused. When managing 80 logins is a chore, people recycle the same few.

That last point leads to credential stuffing: attackers take passwords leaked in one breach and try them on other services. It’s one of the most common ways accounts get taken over.

What a Business Password Manager Does

Think of it as a locked, organised vault for every login your team uses, with clear rules about who can open which door.

Generates strong, unique passwords for every account
Stores everything encrypted
Shares logins safely, without pasting passwords into chat
Controls access by role or team
Keeps an audit log of who accessed what, and when
Removes a leaver’s access in seconds
Autofills logins, which makes life easier for your team
How to Choose One

Use this checklist when comparing tools:

[ ] Zero-knowledge encryption (even the provider can’t read your data)
[ ] Multi-factor authentication (MFA) support
[ ] Team sharing and role-based permissions
[ ] Admin controls and activity reports
[ ] Simple onboarding and offboarding
[ ] Breach or dark web monitoring
[ ] Works on the devices and browsers your team really uses

Price matters, but weigh it against the cost of one breach: downtime, lost customer trust, recovery work, and possible legal trouble.

Getting Your Team on Board

The most common worry is “my team will hate another tool.” In practice, the right password manager saves time. No more hunting for portal passwords, no more reset emails on Monday mornings.

Start with one team, set it up properly, and let the results speak. Once people see it’s easier than the spreadsheet, they stop going back to it.

Pair It With MFA

A password manager isn’t magic. Turn on multi-factor authentication for the vault itself and for critical accounts such as email, banking, and admin panels. If a password is ever exposed, MFA gives attackers one more wall to climb.

Your One-Week Action Plan
[ ] Find every sticky note, notebook, and spreadsheet that holds passwords
[ ] List your most critical accounts: email, banking, cloud storage, admin tools
[ ] Choose a business password manager and move those accounts in first
[ ] Securely delete the old spreadsheet (don’t just archive it)
[ ] Turn on MFA for the vault and key accounts
[ ] Give the team a short, friendly walkthrough
